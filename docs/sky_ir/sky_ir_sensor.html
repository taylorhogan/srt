Iris Sky Sensor

An infrared cloud sensor for the roof: an MLX90614 looking at the zenith, a BME280 for the air, an ESP32 running ESPHome, read over local HTTP like the Shelly relays. Clear sky reads 20–35 °C colder than the air; cloud closes that gap.

2 · Wiring schematic

5 V / 3V3GNDSDA · GPIO8SCL · GPIO9

Pin map

3V3
MLX VDD, BME280 VIN, both pull-ups
GND
MLX, BME280, MOSFET source, 10 kΩ pull-down
GPIO8
SDA, both sensors
GPIO9
SCL, both sensors
GPIO10
heater gate, through 220 Ω
5V
USB 5 V through the board's diode; feeds the heater only

Notes

  • These are the ESP32-S3 pins (DevKitC-1, N16R8). GPIO 22 to 25 do not exist on the S3; on a classic ESP32-DevKitC the same wiring is SDA 21, SCL 22, heater 25. Pins 26 to 37 are the S3 module's flash and PSRAM, 19/20 are USB, 0/3/45/46 are strapping pins: leave them all alone.
  • The MLX runs at 3 V; never put its VDD or SDA/SCL on the 5 V rail.
  • Keep the I²C leads under 15 cm. The can sits in the lid, the board underneath.
  • One ground. The heater's 0.1 A returns through the same GND as the sensors; keep that lead short so the drop stays off the I²C ground reference.
  • The MLX reports two temperatures: the object (sky) and its own die. Use the BME280 for the air; the die runs warm once the heater is on.

3 · Enclosure

  1. Drill the lid for the can (9 mm) and the vent cap; fit the can from inside with its leads soldered to 10 cm of hook-up wire, seal the rim with silicone, and let it cure before the heater goes on.
  2. Bond the heater resistor to the side of the can with thermal epoxy. Conformal-coat the perfboard once it works.
  3. Mount the box tilted 10° toward the gland side. Site it where the 35° cone sees only sky: the north side of the roof structure on a short mast, clear of the roof's travel, and not downwind of the dehumidifier's exhaust.
  4. Reserve the ESP32's address in DHCP, as for Iris North, so the Python side has a fixed host.

4 · ESPHome configuration

The whole firmware. Compile and flash it with the ESPHome tool on the observatory PC (the steps are in docs/SKY_IR_PC_PLAN.md), the lead in the port marked COM (or UART) the first time; later edits go over the air. The browser installer alone only writes a generic firmware. The heater switches on when the air is damp or the can is near the dew point.

esphome:
  name: sky-ir
esp32:
  board: esp32-s3-devkitc-1
  variant: esp32s3
  flash_size: 16MB
  framework:
    type: esp-idf
wifi:
  ssid: !secret wifi_ssid
  password: !secret wifi_password
web_server:
  port: 80           # http://sky-ir/sensor/sky_temp -> {"value": -24.3, ...}
logger:                # logs on the COM port, where esphome run reads them
ota:
  - platform: esphome
    password: !secret ota_password

i2c:
  sda: 8
  scl: 9
  frequency: 100kHz

sensor:
  - platform: mlx90614
    address: 0x5A
    update_interval: 10s
    ambient:
      name: ir_case_temp          # the die, warm when the heater runs
      id: case_t
    object:
      name: sky_temp
      id: sky_t
      filters:
        - sliding_window_moving_average: {window_size: 6, send_every: 6}
  - platform: bme280_i2c
    address: 0x76
    update_interval: 30s
    temperature: {name: air_temp, id: air_t}
    humidity:    {name: humidity, id: rh}
    pressure:    {name: pressure}
  - platform: template
    name: sky_delta               # the cloud metric: sky minus air
    unit_of_measurement: "°C"
    update_interval: 30s
    lambda: return id(sky_t).state - id(air_t).state;
  - platform: template
    name: dew_point
    id: dew
    unit_of_measurement: "°C"
    update_interval: 30s
    lambda: |-
      float a = 17.62, b = 243.12, t = id(air_t).state, h = id(rh).state;
      float g = log(h / 100.0) + a * t / (b + t);
      return b * g / (a - g);

output:
  - platform: gpio
    pin: 10
    id: heater_pin
switch:
  - platform: output
    name: heater
    id: heater
    output: heater_pin

interval:
  - interval: 60s
    then:
      - if:
          condition:
            lambda: return id(rh).state > 85 || id(case_t).state < id(dew).state + 2;
          then: [switch.turn_on: heater]
          else: [switch.turn_off: heater]

5 · Reading it

sky_delta (sky − air)SkyConfidence
−35 … −20 °CClearHigh in dry air; a humid clear night reads a few degrees warmer.
−20 … −10 °CThin cloud or hazeThe band to calibrate against the camera's transparency.
−10 … −3 °CCloudHigh.
−3 … 0 °COvercast, fog, or dew on the canFail-safe either way; the heater and humidity tell fog from dew.

Do not hard-code those thresholds. Log sky_delta, air_temp and humidity for two weeks beside the camera's transparency in local/sky_log.jsonl (plate-solve matches over catalogue stars, V ≤ 5, moon- and twilight-screened) and fit the bands from the data, with humidity and air temperature as terms. That fit is what the commercial units ship as factory constants.

Into SRT

  • hardware_control/sky_ir.py: one HTTP poll a minute of /sensor/sky_delta, /sensor/air_temp, /sensor/humidity, /sensor/dew_point; appended to sky_log.jsonl as a second truth stream.
  • Feeds weather_watch.verdict for the mid-night close (F1), two weeks in shadow first, as planned.
  • status shows the delta and the sky word; a drop of more than 10 °C in 15 minutes is a priority-1 push.
  • Day and night labels for the sky-camera cloud classifier, independent of stars and the moon.

Failure reads

  • Unreachable: unknown, never clear. The conductor treats unknown as a refusal, as it does for every sensor.
  • Dew on the can reads as overcast. Correct direction; the heater prevents the record from being ruined.
  • A warm roof edge in the cone reads as permanent thin cloud. Move the mast, not the threshold.
  • Snow on the can reads 0 °C delta all day. The BME280 at the same time says the air is below freezing with high humidity; a day of flat delta in that state is a “go and look” push.
